# Long-form: YouTube, Vimeo, event screens

Horizontal (16:9) is the default. The principles of `documentary.md` and `craft.md`
apply in full; this file adds what changes when the viewer has chosen to watch for
longer.

## Structure

- **Cold open (10 to 30 s).** The strongest moment or the central question, before
  any title. Common practice on YouTube is to state what the film is and deliver a
  first payoff inside the first 30 seconds, where audience retention graphs usually
  drop most; check the channel's own retention graph rather than a rule of thumb.
- **Title and place.** A short title card, then the establishing sequence: where
  and when, in pictures (drone, wide, signs, crowd arriving).
- **Acts with peaks.** Use the 24 / 54 / 80 / 95% peak map from `documentary.md`.
  Each act ends on a change, not on a repeat of the last.
- **Breathers.** After every peak, a quieter passage (portraits, nat sound, a slower
  music section) so the next peak can land.
- **Ending.** The outcome, a short denouement, then credits or an end screen.

## Pace

- Shots run longer than on social: two to six seconds is normal for action, longer
  for interviews and portraits.
- Montage sections cut to music can run at short-form pace; vary them with story
  sections cut on sound and meaning.
- An interview bite is cut on sense, not on the beat. Use B-roll over a bite only
  when the picture adds something the face does not.
- A music bed for ten minutes is several tracks, or one track cut into sections
  with silences between. Wall-to-wall music flattens everything.

## YouTube specifics

- **Chapters.** Timestamps in the description, starting at 0:00, help viewers
  navigate longer films. Plan the edit so chapter boundaries are real section
  changes.
- **End screen.** YouTube end-screen elements go in the last 5 to 20 seconds. Leave
  that space free of essential picture and text.
- **Shorts from the same edit.** Cut 9:16 versions of the strongest sections as
  separate Shorts. Re-edit them for the format: a new hook and new framing. A
  centre-cropped long-form scene is not a Short.

## Vimeo and event screens

- Vimeo audiences watch full-screen and attentively. A slower, more cinematic pace
  and higher bitrate pay off.
- On a screen at an event there is no sound control, there are viewers at the back
  of the hall, and nobody can scroll back. Use big type, long holds on text, no fine
  detail, and check the venue's playback format and frame rate before export.

## Horizontal from vertical material

9:16 originals in a 16:9 film need a design decision, not a default:

- a blurred or graphic fill behind the vertical frame
- a split screen with two or three vertical shots side by side
- a frame or device graphic

Choose one and use it consistently, or leave the vertical material out.

# Short-form: Reels, TikTok, Shorts

How evidence is marked here:

- **[platform]** means the platform's own help pages say it.
- **[practice]** means editors and creators widely do it, and it has not been
  measured for you.

Do not present practice as a platform rule. Test it on the account's own
analytics.

---

## Formats and limits

| | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | up to 3 min, square or vertical **[platform]** | uploads up to 3 min in 9:16 or 1:1 are classed as Shorts; upload 16:9 to avoid that |
| Instagram Reels | up to 20 min **[platform]** | Reels over 3 min are not recommended to new audiences **[platform]** |
| TikTok | check the current help page | limits change often; do not hard-code them |

Deliver 1080×1920 at the project rate (25 or 30, or the camera rate). Export from
the originals, not from an earlier export (`delivery.md`).

## Interface margins

The platforms publish no fixed organic safe zone. Meta's guidance for Reels ads is
the best published reference: keep key content clear of the top 14%, the bottom
35% and 6% at each side, which at 1080×1920 is about 270 px top, 670 px bottom and
65 px each side **[platform, ads]**. Treat it as the conservative margin for text
and faces. A looser margin (top 250, bottom 320) keeps text clear of most organic
interface, but captions and the like/comment column can still cover it.

## The hook: the first 1 to 3 seconds

TikTok's own creative guidance stresses the first three seconds and a clear,
on-message hook **[platform, ads]**.

- **Open on the strongest moment you have**: a face, an impact, a number, a
  question. Never on a logo or a slow establishing shot.
- **The hook must be true to the film.** It promises what follows; a hook the body
  never pays off loses trust.
- **Hook archetypes** to offer as options, each with a line, an on-screen text and a
  picture:

| Archetype | Shape |
|---|---|
| In media res | open mid-action; the picture is the hook |
| Curiosity gap | say an answer exists, withhold it |
| Number | a concrete figure in the first line (a time, a weight, a distance) |
| Stakes | what it cost, or what was at stake |
| Direct call-out | name the exact viewer ("Were you at the finish line on Sunday?") |
| Contrarian | a defensible position against the obvious |
| Social proof | real demand or a real crowd |

- **For an event film the place can come right after the hook.** Hook (one to three
  seconds), then the establishing shot, then the build (`craft.md` §3).

## Retention

- **[practice]** Reset the frame often: a new subject, a new scale or a new angle
  every one to two seconds in a beat edit, every two to four in a story edit.
- **[practice]** Escalate. Each third of the piece should hit harder than the one
  before.
- **[practice]** Put the payoff late, but not at the very end. The last few seconds
  are for the outcome and a closing image, then the end card.
- **[platform]** YouTube counts starts and replays as Shorts views. Judge a cut on
  watch time and engaged views, not on raw view counts.

## Loops

**[practice]** A loop is a cut that makes the end flow into the start: the last
shot matches the first in action, framing or sound. It can lift replays, but no
platform publishes that it lifts distribution. Make one when the material offers it
naturally, and A/B it if the account cares. A loop is never a substitute for an
ending (`documentary.md` §7).

Ways to loop:

- the last frame continues the first shot's movement
- the music's last bar leads into its first
- the closing line completes the opening line

## Text and captions

- **[platform, ads]** TikTok recommends captions for clarity.
- **[practice]** Make the premise readable without sound. There is no reliable
  current figure for how many viewers watch muted, so do not quote one; design for
  mute anyway.
- One job per card: identify, state one fact, bridge, or give the outcome.
- About three to eight words a line, held long enough to read twice. Never on a
  face.
- Burn text in only on request. Otherwise hand it over with its timings so it can be
  placed per shot in the editor.

## Captions and the question at the end

- **[practice]** Ask one concrete, cheap question at the end. "Guess the time"
  beats "thoughts?".
- **[practice]** Ask an identity question in communities with an in-group: "Which
  unit are you from?"
- Avoid engagement bait ("comment YES if"). Platforms down-rank it.

## Concepts for a series

Aim each piece at one job: reach (works for strangers), community (rewards
insiders), proof (a number or moment that settles it), or conversion (one action).
A slate where every piece does the same job is one idea in five versions.

# Concept, hooks and captions

## Hooks

The first 1 to 3 seconds decide whether anything else is seen. Treat hook writing
as the main event.

Produce **six to ten hooks across different archetypes**, not six rewordings of
one. Label each with its archetype so the person learns the pattern rather than
just picking a line. Give the spoken line, the on-screen text, and the visual —
a hook that only works as text is half a hook.

| Archetype | Shape | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | name that an answer exists, withhold it | the payoff is genuinely non-obvious |
| Stakes | frame the cost of not watching | advice, education |
| Direct call-out | name the exact viewer | narrows reach, spikes relevance |
| Contrarian | stake a position against consensus | must be defensible in the payoff |
| In media res | open mid-action, explain after | the visual *is* the hook |
| Number | a concrete figure in the first line | proof, records, specs |
| Cost confession | what it cost, specifically | the number does the work |
| Social proof | borrow implied demand | you have real demand to borrow |

**Lead with the cost or the stake, never with the brand.** "Check out our new…"
asks for attention nobody owes you. A number, a weight, a time, or a consequence
earns it.

Say it out loud. If it takes longer than about two seconds or stumbles, cut it.

### Anti-patterns

Slow build-ups, logos, "hey guys". Vague benefit with no concrete noun. Cleverness
that needs explaining. A hook the body never pays off — the fastest way to lose
trust.

## Structure

A 30-second piece is three 10-second pieces stacked; each has to earn the next.

```
0-3s    hook          the whole game
3-10s   setup         one idea, escalating
10-25s  payoff        deliver what the hook promised
last 3s CTA or loop   natural, not bolted on
```

Cut every 2 to 4 seconds. Reset framing or subject on each cut. A static frame
longer than about four seconds needs a reason.

**Loop where you can.** Ending on the frame you opened with turns one view into
several and costs nothing.

## Captions and CTAs

**One question per piece, at the end.** Concrete and cheap to answer beats open-
ended. "Guess the time" outperforms "what do you think?" because it takes two
seconds and has a right answer to argue about.

Identity questions are the strongest of all in a community with an in-group:
asking which unit, team, town or year someone is from turns a comment section into
a roll call people return to read.

**Write for mute.** Most first views have no sound. If the piece only works with
audio, it only works for a minority.

**Avoid engagement bait.** "Comment YES if…" and "share to win" get throttled.
A real question does not.

## Concepts

Sketch three to five, each one sentence, each aimed at a different job:

- **Reach** — works for someone who knows nothing about the subject
- **Conversion** — one clear action, one clear reason
- **Community** — rewards insiders, drives shares within a group
- **Proof** — a number or a moment that settles an argument

A slate where all five do the same job is one concept with four variations.
